The math under the feathers

Every lane has a survival probability, and the multiplier for reaching it is roughly the inverse of your cumulative chance of getting there, trimmed by the house margin. That trim is the whole model: whether you bank after one lane or ten, the long-run expected value of the round stays the same few percent below your stake.

The practical result: there is no mathematically superior stopping point. Stopping early trades ceiling for frequency; stopping late does the reverse — a choice about variance and temperament, never about beating the margin in chicken road online play.
